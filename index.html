Pitch Deck Check

Checks a PowerPoint deck against the published rules of the Public Service Data/AI Challenge pitch training (November 19, 2026). It situates the deck against those rules; it does not score or rank it. Judges decide.

Your file never leaves your computer. It is read in this browser tab only. Nothing is uploaded, stored or sent anywhere. No artificial intelligence is used: every check is a fixed rule you can read below.

Automatic checks

Each verdict is written in words, never shown by colour alone. Fits, Partly and Does not fit are measured from the file; To check needs a person.

Rules checked, and their source

  • Text 18 pt or larger; high contrast; never colour alone; clean slides. (Accessibility considerations)
  • Avoid jargon and acronyms, on the slides and in the speech; simple slides, details in annexes. (Key considerations)
  • Do not include an outline; use the full 10 minutes; first 60 seconds: the problem and why it matters. (Pitch structure)
  • Name challenges and obstacles; data access; tools; users; who has been consulted; next steps and risks. (What the judges look for)
  • Six judging criteria. (Judging criteria)

Source: Pitch Training for the Public Service Data/AI Challenge, November 19, 2026. This page is an independent aid, not an official tool of the challenge. Limits: font sizes inherited from a slide layout cannot be measured from the file and are marked “To check”; contrast and colour must be checked on screen. See the Methodology tab.

What this tool is for

To let each team check its own deck against the published rules, and improve it, without waiting for an outside opinion. It is meant to be good enough to be useful, not perfect. It is a mirror, not a judge.

Reading rules: how to use the verdicts

  • A verdict is a signal, not a mark. “Does not fit” means the file shows something the rule asks you to avoid. Read the detail, then decide.
  • “Fits” is not a guarantee. It means the code found nothing, not that a judge will agree. Some things are invisible to the code.
  • “To check” is a task for a person, best done on the projector you will use.
  • False positives exist. A word in capitals is not always an acronym; a bracket is not always a placeholder. Dismiss them knowingly.
  • The self-assessment is yours. Ticking a box records what you believe the deck shows; it is not checked by the code.
  • No score, no ranking. The verdicts are never added up. Two decks cannot be compared with this tool.
  • The rules are the organizers’. If this page and the organizers ever differ, the organizers are right.

Honest limits

  • Not validated on a corpus. The checks were tested on two real pitch decks and the organizers’ training deck, and compared with a manual review of one of them. They were not tested on a set of, say, a hundred decks with known defects and known clean ones. There is therefore no measured rate of false alarms or missed problems.
  • Known blind spots.
    • Font sizes inherited from a layout are not measured.
    • Text inside images, charts, SmartArt and embedded objects is not read.
    • Contrast, colour use, legibility from the back of the room and the quality of the story are not assessed.
    • The spoken script is known only through the speaker notes; what is actually said may differ.
    • Links are not opened, so a broken link is not detected.
    • Acronyms are recognized by shape only, not checked against a definition given aloud.
  • Thresholds are choices. 70 words per slide and 9 minutes as “using the full time” are our reading of the guidance, not numbers given by the organizers.
  • Files that may break it. Very old or unusual files, password-protected files and files saved by other tools than PowerPoint may fail to open or give partial results.
  • English first. The patterns are written for English decks; French decks are only partly covered (outline titles, some cues).

Transparency and ownership

The code is open source under the MIT licence. Every rule is readable in analyse.js. Each team may run its own copy, offline, and adapt it. Nobody hosts your file, and nobody maintains a service for you. Improvements are welcome, and should keep the rule, its source and its limits visible.
